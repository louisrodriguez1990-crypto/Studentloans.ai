Federal student loan forgiveness is real — but different programs have very different requirements, timelines, and current statuses. Here's what you need to know about each path in 2026.

**As of April 2026:** The SAVE Plan is blocked by a court injunction. Borrowers in SAVE are in administrative forbearance — payments are paused and do not count toward forgiveness. Other forgiveness paths remain active but have specific eligibility requirements.

---

## Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF)

**What it is:** After 10 years (120 qualifying payments) of working for a government or qualifying nonprofit employer, your remaining federal Direct Loan balance is forgiven — tax-free.

**Who qualifies:**
- Employees of federal, state, local, or tribal government agencies
- Employees of 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations
- Certain other public service roles (check StudentAid.gov for the full list)

**Loan requirements:** Must have federal Direct Loans (or have consolidated eligible loans into a Direct Consolidation Loan). FFEL and Perkins Loans don't qualify unless consolidated.

**Repayment plan requirements:** Must be on an income-driven repayment plan while making qualifying payments.

**Current status (April 2026):** PSLF itself is functioning normally. The SAVE Plan injunction affects SAVE-enrolled borrowers' payment counts but doesn't affect PSLF for borrowers on IBR or PAYE.

**Best for:** Borrowers with high debt relative to income who work in public service and plan to stay for 10 years.

---

## Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) Forgiveness

**What it is:** After 20 or 25 years of qualifying payments on an income-driven repayment plan, your remaining balance is forgiven. Unlike PSLF, this forgiveness has historically been taxable as income (though the Biden-era tax exemption through 2025 may affect recent forgiveness events).

**Timeline:**
- **IBR (for new borrowers after July 1, 2014):** 20 years
- **IBR (for older borrowers):** 25 years
- **PAYE:** 20 years
- **SAVE (when active):** 10 years for balances under $12,000; 20–25 years for larger balances

**Current status (April 2026):**
- **IBR:** Active and accepting new enrollees. Payments count toward forgiveness.
- **PAYE:** Closed to new enrollees as of July 1, 2024.
- **SAVE:** Blocked by court injunction. Borrowers in SAVE are in forbearance. Payments do not count toward forgiveness during forbearance.

**Best for:** Borrowers with high debt relative to income who don't work in qualifying public service, or who want a forgiveness backstop regardless of employer.

---

## Teacher Loan Forgiveness

**What it is:** Up to $17,500 in forgiveness for teachers who complete five consecutive years of full-time teaching in a low-income school or educational service agency.

**Loan requirements:** Must have Direct Loans or FFEL Loans taken out before you began the five-year teaching period.

**Limitation:** You cannot count the same teaching years toward both Teacher Loan Forgiveness and PSLF. Most teachers with high balances benefit more from pursuing PSLF.

**Best for:** Teachers with smaller balances who may not stay in teaching long enough for PSLF.

---

## Borrower Defense to Repayment

**What it is:** If your school misled you or violated state law in connection with your loan, you may be able to have your loans discharged entirely.

**Current status (April 2026):** Applications are being processed but the program has faced administrative changes. Check StudentAid.gov for current processing timelines.

**Best for:** Borrowers who attended schools that closed or engaged in deceptive practices.

---

## Closed School Discharge

**What it is:** If your school closed while you were enrolled (or shortly after you withdrew), you may be eligible for a full discharge of your loans.

**Best for:** Borrowers affected by school closures.

## Choosing the right path

| Factor | PSLF | IDR Forgiveness | Refinancing (no forgiveness) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer | Govt/nonprofit required | Any | Any |
| Timeline | 10 years | 20–25 years | Pay off fully |
| Tax on forgiveness | Tax-free | Potentially taxable | N/A |
| Payment amount | IDR-based (low) | IDR-based (low) | Fixed (may be lower rate) |
| Current status | Active | IBR active; SAVE blocked | Available |

**The most common mistake:** Assuming you can pursue PSLF while in SAVE. With SAVE's payments currently paused, borrowers targeting PSLF should switch to IBR to keep accumulating qualifying payments.
